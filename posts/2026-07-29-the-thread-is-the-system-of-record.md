title: The thread is the system of record
date: 2026-07-29

Every telecom field crew I worked on documented the job twice.

Once properly — the forms, the templates, the closeout package the operator asked for. And once in
the group chat, where somebody photographed the cabinet before they closed it because they knew
they'd need it later, and somebody else typed *azimuth is 40 not 60* at eleven at night.

The second one was always the accurate one. It was written while the crew was still standing on
the site, by the people who could see the thing being described. The first was written afterwards,
from memory, by someone reconstructing a day they had already stopped thinking about.

We treated the chat as the informal channel and the package as the real record. It was the other
way round the whole time.

## What that costs

Closeout is where the money is. A package gets accepted or it comes back, and when it comes back
the crew has already left the site, so somebody drives out again or somebody guesses. Neither is
free. Payment waits on acceptance, and acceptance waits on documentation that was assembled from
the weaker of two sources.

The chat had the evidence. It just wasn't in a shape anybody could submit.

## The uncomfortable part

The obvious move is to replace the chat with an app. Give the crew a proper tool, structured
fields, a submit button. I've watched that fail more than once, and the reason isn't that the apps
are bad.

It's that the chat already works. It's on a phone the crew already carries, in an app they already
have open, with no login, no training, and no reason to think about it. Anything that asks a crew
to stop doing the thing that works and start doing a thing that's better in principle is asking
them to absorb a cost today for a benefit that lands on someone else's desk next week.

So don't replace it. Read it.

The interesting problem isn't building a better place for field crews to put information. It's
that they already put it somewhere, correctly, at the right moment — and everything downstream
throws it away.
